# Translation and Register

Read [the entrypoint](../SKILL.md).

## Translation sheet

One row per spoken line: line id, speaker, addressee, source text, target text,
register note, length check, status (`draft`, `user_approved`, `native_checked`)
and notes. The sheet lives in the project; the source column is the locked text
and is never edited.

## Register

- Record formality per speaker pair and keep it in the target language: boss and
  employee, elder and youth, spouses, strangers. Do not flatten pairs into one
  voice.
- Carry honorifics, family terms and politeness markers the target language has
  for the same relationship. If the target language has no equivalent, rewrite
  the relationship cue in the line and record the choice.
- Keep the intent of each beat: the hook accusation stays an accusation, the
  reveal line still lands as a reveal, the cliffhanger question stays open.
- Prefer short, speakable sentences. One breath per line.

## Length fit

Spoken duration is bounded by the delivered window. Estimate the target line at
natural speed and compare it with the source line's measured duration. A line
that needs more than about 15 percent extra time is rewritten shorter, not
accelerated. Reading speed for subtitles follows the sidecar limits.

## Names, terms and glossary

- Keep character names as in the source unless the user supplies target forms.
- Record words that must stay untranslated and words with fixed translations in
  a short glossary reused across episodes of a series.
- Do not borrow idioms, honorifics or settings from another market's example.

## Review and unverified items

Machine or agent translation is a draft. Cultural fit, slang, taboo words,
religious or political references and dialect choice are flagged `unverified`
until a native speaker confirms. List them for the user; do not guess.
